Egypt as people imagine it, and Egypt as people live in it now

Useful information • Hurghada • ≈ 4 min read

There is a persistent stereotype: Egypt is a “wild” country where people live in boxes, with dirt and crime everywhere. That is why many people either do not consider it for a holiday, or, once they arrive, never step outside the hotel fence. But reality, especially in Hurghada, has changed radically. Today it is a city with infrastructure that makes not only a short holiday but a full life comfortable.

The myth of “boxes” and the absence of civilisation

The misconception of total ruin comes from tourists who see only empty lots and unfinished buildings on the way from the airport. But next to these areas there are often modern residential complexes and a developed urban environment. Hurghada long ago stopped being just a “resort area” — it is a full-fledged city with a European level of everyday comfort.

Just imagine: the Amazon marketplace operates in Hurghada, there is grocery delivery from Spinneys and Carrefour (via their own apps or the Instashop aggregator), and food from cafés and restaurants can be ordered through Talabat. For getting around the city, Uber and InDrive are available — a ride across town costs from 30 to 60 Egyptian pounds, which makes taxis very affordable.

Shopping is no longer a problem: Hurghada City Center (with a cinema and shops) and Senzo Mall cover most everyday needs. And having an IKEA in Senzo Mall is perhaps one of the clearest markers that Hurghada has become a city to live in, not just a place for “all inclusive”.

The internet in Hurghada has also stopped being a problem: both mobile and wired connections are available, which lets you work remotely and stay in touch.

Living in an apartment, not a hotel: why it pays off

Because of these changes, more and more people choose not a hotel but renting or buying an apartment. And it is not only a question of saving money, although the prices really are impressive: the minimum rent for a two-room apartment starts from $100 a month (a one-bedroom or a studio can be found cheaper).

Living in an apartment gives you freedom: there is no tie to the hotel dining room schedule, you can eat when and where you like — in the many city cafés or by ordering food delivery home. And Hurghada has many beaches: besides hotel beaches, there are well-equipped public beaches with affordable entry and infrastructure. You can rent a place near the sea or even with its own beach.

Egypt has stopped being a country you have to fear or hide from behind a hotel fence. Hurghada is a city with Spinneys delivery, taxis via InDrive, shopping malls and IKEA. Life here has become truly comfortable, and more and more people choose not a hotel room but an apartment by the sea.
