Detailed Guide to Hurghada Neighborhoods: Property Prices, Beaches, Cleanliness, and Safety

Useful Information • Hurghada • ≈ 12 min read

Hurghada has long outgrown its status as a simple Egyptian resort town, evolving into a diverse coastal metropolis on the Red Sea. Here, traditional Egyptian lifestyle, bustling tourist avenues, cozy European enclaves, and luxurious gated resorts coexist side by side[cite: 3].

We explore 8 key neighborhoods in Hurghada: from buying and rental prices to stray animals, beach specifics, and gated security access.

1. El Kawsar (El Kawther)

The Main Expat and European Hub of Hurghada

  • Environment & Cleanliness: One of the cleanest and best-developed areas in the city. Paved streets, abundant greenery, streetlights, and sidewalk paving tiles. Minimal ongoing construction. Stray dogs are rare; most pets are kept on leashes.
  • Residents: Primarily Europeans (Germans, British, Scandinavians) and Russian-speaking expats. Well-to-do Egyptian families also choose Kawsar for its safety and developed environment.
  • Beaches: No direct access to the sea; located on the second line behind the Mamsha promenade. A 10–15 minute walk to the beach. Paid hotel beaches are available (Mashrabiya, Old Vic, Dream Beach) — day passes cost $5 to $15. No free public beaches nearby.
  • Infrastructure: Excellent. Supermarkets (Metro, Bestway, Abu Ashara), international medical clinics, fitness centers (Gold’s Gym, Viking), language kindergartens, international schools, and European/Eastern restaurants within walking distance.
  • Prestige Rating: High (8/10). Safe, European vibe, no aggressive street vendors.
  • Prices:
    • Rent: 1-bedroom apartment — $250–400/month; 2-bedroom — $400–650/month.
    • Purchase: $600–900 per sq. m (1-bedroom apartment — $35,000–55,000).
  • Transport: Car optional. Everything is nearby, and Uber rides or public minibuses (servicers) cost a couple of dollars.

2. Sheraton / Sakkala

The Heart of Nightlife and Tourist Energy

  • Environment & Cleanliness: The most vibrant, bustling, and colorful area. Sheraton Street is active day and night: signs, shops, street touts, heavy traffic. Moderate cleanliness — regular cleaning on the main street, but backyards may have litter, construction debris, and stray dogs.
  • Residents: Mixed population. Long-term expats, tourists, tourism workers, Egyptian merchants, and local business owners.
  • Beaches: Paid beaches within walking distance (El Sakia, Anemone, Orange Beach). Day pass $2–$5. Around Hurghada Marina, beaches are private or premium, but the promenade is fantastic. No free swimming areas.
  • Infrastructure: Fully equipped. Dozens of cafes, fish market, port, clothing stores, chain gyms, and pharmacies.
  • Prestige Rating: Moderate (5/10). Great tourist potential, but the noise, touts, and constant hustle are not for everyone.
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $200–350/month for a 1-bedroom apartment.
    • Purchase: $500–750 per sq. m.
  • Transport: Car not needed. Taxis and minibuses are readily available.

3. El Hadaba

A Quiet Residential Hill Between Downtown and Mamsha

  • Environment & Cleanliness: Located on elevated terrain. Diverse character: peaceful streets lined with villas and European-style compounds alongside older Egyptian quarters with vacant lots and stray animals.
  • Residents: Around 50% foreigners (Russian-speaking, Germans, Italians), with the rest being Egyptian middle-class families.
  • Beaches: Seafront occupied by cliffs and hotel resorts. Residents use paid beaches in Sheraton (El Sakia, Old Vic), a 10-to-20-minute walk downhill.
  • Infrastructure: Complete urban setup. Small supermarkets, gyms, fruit stands, and beauty salons. Main Kawsar and Sheraton hubs are a 5-minute taxi ride away.
  • Prestige Rating: Moderate-High (6.5/10). Quiet, few tourists, minimal street touting.
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $200–350/month.
    • Purchase: $450–650 per sq. m.
  • Transport: Personal car is convenient due to hill slopes, though Uber fares into town remain very low.

4. Madares

Dynamic Residential Area with Authentic Local Character

  • Environment & Cleanliness: Madares ("School Street") is a densely populated artery connecting Hadaba, Sheraton, and Dahar. Home to multiple schools, bringing lively daytime traffic and children. Courtyards can be dusty with unfinished structures and stray animals.
  • Residents: About 70% local Egyptian families, alongside budget-conscious long-term expats.
  • Beaches: No local beaches. Nearest paid beaches in Sheraton or Arabia require a 5–10 minute commute.
  • Infrastructure: Excellent local amenities: fresh meat and vegetable markets, bakeries, stationery stores, pharmacies, and small neighborhood gyms.
  • Prestige Rating: Below Average (4.5/10). Authentic, unpolished Hurghada — friendly and affordable.
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $150–250/month.
    • Purchase: $350–500 per sq. m.
  • Transport: Car not required; cheap local minibuses run frequently along the main street.

5. El Dahar

Authentic Old Town Quarter

  • Environment & Cleanliness: The historic core of Hurghada. Narrow alleys, traditional souk, spice aromas, and vibrant street life. Dusty in parts, with sandy lots and street animals.
  • Residents: Nearly 90% local Egyptian population. Foreigners are rare (mostly students, long-term divers, or ultra-budget travelers).
  • Beaches: Public and hotel beaches available (e.g., Public Beach No. 4, Triton/Hilton Plaza beaches). Free access zones are unmanaged and frequented mostly by locals.
  • Infrastructure: Home to the city’s cheapest vegetable and fish market (Dahar Market), government administration, hospitals, and traditional coffee shops. Gyms are modest.
  • Prestige Rating: Low (3/10) for European long-term living, but high for cultural immersion.
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $120–220/month.
    • Purchase: $300–450 per sq. m.
  • Transport: Driving can be difficult due to narrow streets and parking challenges. Taxis or minibuses are preferred.

6. El Ahyaa (Al Ahseya)

Northern Developing Residential Compound Zone

  • Environment & Cleanliness: Spacious, long coastal strip stretching toward El Gouna. Composed of newly built gated compounds and private villas. Open spaces make it feel breezy and wide, though ongoing construction remains common.
  • Residents: High concentration of international expats (Russian-speaking, European) who bought affordable seaside apartments, plus middle-income Egyptian families.
  • Beaches: Key highlight: wide free wild beaches. However, shallow tides mean waters recede significantly during low tide, so many compounds feature long piers/pontoon bridges.
  • Infrastructure: Rapidly expanding. Supermarkets (Bestway), French and international schools, and cafes are open. Main shopping and Gold’s Gym facilities require a trip to downtown or El Gouna.
  • Prestige Rating: Moderate (5.5/10). Quiet, developing coastal suburb.
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $150–300/month.
    • Purchase: $350–600 per sq. m.
  • Transport: Personal vehicle highly recommended. Distances are large, and taxis into downtown can add up.

7. Sahl Hasheesh

Exclusive Gated Luxury Resort in the South

  • Environment & Cleanliness: Premium gated enclave featuring a 12 km seaside boardwalk, fountains, palm avenues, and golf carts. Crime and stray animals are nonexistent due to strict perimeter security. Pristine landscaping and cleanliness.
  • Residents: Wealthy Europeans, affluent Egyptians from Cairo, premium expats, and luxury villa owners.
  • Access: Restricted. Security checkpoints inspect vehicles at entry gates. Access granted to residents, resort guests, or visitors with confirmed reservations.
  • Beaches: Stunning sandy beaches with turquoise water and house reefs. Beaches are managed by resorts and compounds, with open access for residents.
  • Infrastructure: Fine dining restaurants, luxury spas, golf courses, piers, and boutique markets. Senzo Mall is a 10–15 minute drive away.
  • Prestige Rating: Exceptional (9.5/10).
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $600–1,500+/month.
    • Purchase: $1,200–2,500+ per sq. m.
  • Transport: Personal car is essential. Isolated from public transit networks.

8. El Gouna

"The Venice of Egypt" — Autonomous Eco-City

  • Environment & Cleanliness: A private lagoon city engineered by Orascom. Features European elegance: paved roads, custom architecture, recycling systems, strict eco-standards, and no stray animals.
  • Residents: Global elite, tech entrepreneurs, kite surfers, wealthy Europeans, and Egyptian aristocracy.
  • Access: Strictly Controlled. Military and private security checkpoint at the main entrance gate. Entry requires residency pass, hotel booking, or official invitation.
  • Beaches: Lagoon beaches at doorstep level, plus open sea beaches (Moods, Elements, Zeytouna). World-renowned kite-surfing hub. Beaches are private or paid access.
  • Infrastructure: World-class private hospital, international schools and university branch, championship golf courses, airstrip, twin yacht marinas (Downtown & Abu Tig), co-working hubs, and fine dining.
  • Prestige Rating: Maximum (10/10).
  • Prices:
    • Rent: $800–2,500+/month for apartments; $2,500–6,000+ for villas.
    • Purchase: $2,000–4,500+ per sq. m.
  • Transport: Tuk-tuks, bicycles, and electric carts are popular inside El Gouna. Personal car recommended for trips to Hurghada city center (25–30 min).

Summary: Which Area to Choose?

  1. For comfortable family living: El Kawsar or Hadaba.
  2. For budget winter stays near open sea: El Ahyaa.
  3. For vibrant nightlife and entertainment: Sheraton.
  4. For premium privacy and top security: Sahl Hasheesh or El Gouna.
